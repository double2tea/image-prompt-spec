# Discipline

From the Hell Grind feature pipeline and the later reconciliation pass. Platform costs are omitted. The failures are not.

## Before any shot

Lock assets first. A character is a descriptor plus images. A location is a plate plus a geography block. A voice is a line of register, tempo, accent, and manner. An acting paragraph is habits and how they break, not emotion words.

A behavior that cannot fit the scene is transferred, not deleted. The pacer who sits down still taps and sways.

Do not generate the scene until the sheet survives a second angle.

## Iteration

1. Same flaw on the second roll: rewrite the brief.
2. No declared budget and three paid attempts with no improvement: stop and name options.
3. Four video-to-video batches with the same fault: the prompt or the source plate is wrong.
4. Ten to fifteen surgical passes inside a declared budget, then simplify the shot.
5. Sixty-five to a hundred generations per kept shot is a project benchmark, not a target.

Most early Hell Grind waste was a verbatim reroll of a prompt that had already failed.

## Acting

Write what they want, what they hide, the body rhythm, and the visible habit. "Sad" is not a direction. Eyes move. Stillness is held weight, not a freeze.

## Open, do not pretend settled

- Full descriptor next to an identity reference, versus minimal text so the image wins. Both sides agree the text must not contradict the image.
- Clothing edits on GPT versus costume texture on Seedream. Compare.
- Film grain on the identity sheet. Default here: no. The look belongs on the scene plate.
- "Every person moving from frame one" means life, not a scripted beat, during the position-lock wide.
